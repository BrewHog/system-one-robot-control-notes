On publication, priority, and provable authorship

2026-09-18 · about the record itself, not the robotics

This page explains an unusual choice, so that nobody has to guess at my intent: this site is public but deliberately not indexed. It is meant to be citable and archivable by anyone who has the URL, and not surfaced by a general web search.

Why publish at all

I am working on an idea I did not invent. The System 1 / System 2 split for robot control is openly discussed, and there are well-funded teams on it. I make no claim to the concept.

What I do want is a dated, checkable record of my own work on it — the architecture I chose, the numbers I measured, and the bugs I found. If this line of work becomes valuable later, I want the record of when and how I approached it to exist somewhere other than my own laptop, and to be verifiable by a third party without taking my word for anything.

Why not indexed

Two goals that sound contradictory, and are worth separating:

  • Verifiable means anyone with the URL can fetch it, hash it, and confirm it has not changed. That requires it to be genuinely public.
  • Not discoverable means a stranger searching "System 1 robot control" should not land here. That requires not being in a search index.

These are compatible, because indexing and fetching are different things. A page can be freely fetchable while carrying noindex directives and a restrictive robots.txt. Honest caveats, though, which matter more than the mechanism:

  • This is a request, not a guarantee. robots.txt and noindex are directives that well-behaved crawlers honour. They are not access control and cannot stop anyone determined.
  • Archival crawlers are explicitly invited, by design. Preserving a copy is the point of this exercise, so robots.txt allows ia_archiver, archive.org_bot, CCBot and friends while disallowing everyone else.
  • Those two facts interact. Once an archiver stores a copy, that copy is itself a public, indexed page. Archiving for durability and staying out of search results are in genuine tension, and I chose durability. The archiver's copy is likely to be more findable than the original.

If your actual goal is that nobody ever finds it, do not publish it. Every public durability mechanism — archives, repositories, timestamps — is discoverable by design. What I can offer instead is proof, which is a stronger and more durable thing than obscurity, and which is what the rest of this page is about.

How the timestamps work

Four independent layers, deliberately redundant, because any single one of them can fail or be dismissed:

LayerWhat it establishesDepends on
SHA-256 manifest
manifest.txt		 Exactly which bytes were published. Anyone can re-hash and compare. Nothing — pure arithmetic.
OpenTimestamps
manifest.txt.ots		 Those exact bytes existed at or before a specific Bitcoin block height. Verifiable offline, forever. The Bitcoin chain, which is not going away.
RFC 3161 token
manifest.tsr		 An independent timestamping authority signed for the manifest hash at a wall-clock time. That authority's CA remaining verifiable.
Git commit history A second, differently-administered record of the same content and time. The hosting platform.

The important property is that the Bitcoin anchor does not require trusting me, the hosting platform, the timestamp authority, or TypeSafe. It requires only that the blockchain is what it is. That is why it is the backbone of the bundle and the others are corroboration.

How to verify, concretely

# 1. Do the files match what was published?
shasum -a 256 -c manifest.txt

# 2. Is the manifest anchored in Bitcoin?
pip install opentimestamps-client
ots verify manifest.txt.ots

# 3. Inspect the RFC 3161 token
openssl ts -reply -in manifest.tsr -text

The ots verify output names the Bitcoin block the manifest is anchored in. Anyone can independently confirm that block exists and when it was mined. Nothing in that chain passes through me.

Attribution, precisely

If you use or build on this, the credit I am asking for is specific and small:

  • Mine: the control architecture, the test harnesses, the simulator image work, the measurements, the diagnosis of the control-law limit cycle, and the documentation. That is what the manifest hashes.
  • Not mine: ros-mcp-server (Contoro), ROS 2 and turtlesim (Open Robotics / the ROS community), the System 1 model (TypeSafe), and OpenTimestamps. All referenced and credited on the front page. The System 1 / System 2 framing for robotics is a widely discussed idea and I claim no ownership of it.

I am not trying to prevent anyone from working on this. I am trying to make it impossible to claim this particular work was done later, or by someone else, without contradicting a Bitcoin block. That is a modest and achievable goal, and it is the whole reason this site exists in this strange half-public form.
